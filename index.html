Introduction

Short version

Bhavna Sharma is an AI product leader and independent consultant based in Toronto. She spent a decade building products inside organizations. First retail analytics, then enterprise platform work: system migrations run under live load, multi-phase platform consolidations, multilingual content delivery, and data products used across retail, healthcare, pharmacy and food service. She closed that chapter leading a product management team.

In mid-2025 she went independent. She now does two things at once. She helps organizations adopt AI in a way that survives past the demo: training teams, setting up development practice, and deciding what is actually worth building. And she builds herself, hands-on, because judgment about this technology decays quickly if you are not shipping with it.

Her clients are mostly not technology companies. That is deliberate: the most interesting questions about AI are being asked in rooms where nobody has a machine learning team.

Services

How she works with organizations

AI product strategy

Deciding which AI initiatives deserve investment, which to kill, and what happens when the system is wrong. Governance treated as a product problem, not a compliance one.

Team enablement & training

Getting real people genuinely fluent, not tool demos. Workflow design, practice, and the habits that make adoption stick after the enthusiasm fades.

Agentic workflow design

Provider-agnostic agent systems and harnesses, so capability is not hostage to a single vendor's roadmap or pricing.

Prototyping & MVP delivery

Hands-on building to prove a thesis fast, with the discipline to ship the smallest useful version and hold the rest back.

Product craft coaching

Specs, discovery and acceptance criteria for probabilistic systems, where behaviour drifts and failure has to be designed rather than avoided.

Speaking & facilitation

Panels, masterclasses and internal sessions on how the product role is changing, and what stays constant when everything else moves.

Position

What she argues, and why

01

Nobody has figured this out

Not the labs, not the engineers running agents overnight. If you feel behind, you are on time. Start anywhere, one percent at a time.

02

The core remains core

Customers are still customers. There are just more kinds of them now, including the agents. What moved is where judgment lives, and how much of it one person carries.

03

Cheap to build is not a permission slip

Engineering estimates used to do prioritization for us. That constraint is gone, so restraint has become the craft itself.

04

Multiplication, not competition

Anyone who hoards what they know is capped at what one person can learn. Rooms that share compound.

Hearth myhearthapp.ca ↗

A directory for practitioners in Toronto's conscious community: osteopaths, massage therapists and bodyworkers. Built so they could be found. Designed, built, database wired, tested and shipped solo in one week. Practitioners from the community ran the testing themselves: a move worth defending in that context, and worth refusing on anything holding sensitive data.
